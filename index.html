SECTION III

Architectures + training

Foundations for Audio Reasoning Models

Reasoning-capable AFMs need architectures that connect continuous sound with language models, followed by training that aligns modalities and elicits reasoning.

01Large Audio Language Models
AUDIO→ENCODER→PROJECTOR→LLM→TEXT

A pretrained audio encoder extracts features, a projector maps them into the language embedding space, and an LLM generates text.

02Spoken Language Models
AUDIO→SLM→SPEECH discrete speech + text tokens

SLMs add speech generation. They either append a speech decoder or model speech and text tokens in one autoregressive space.

STAGE 01Cross-modal alignment

Connect acoustic patterns to linguistic meaning with ASR and captioning data.

→
STAGE 02Supervised fine-tuning

Teach instructions, task behavior, and optional reasoning traces.

→
STAGE 03Preference optimization / RL

Improve correctness, consistency, and acoustic faithfulness.

SECTION IV

Grounded textual reasoning

Audio-to-Text Reasoning

These AFMs return text while reasoning over speech content, paralinguistic cues, environmental events, and temporal structure.

01 / INFERENCE

Prompting and Grounded Decoding

Audio-CoT elicits intermediate reasoning. SAR-LM exposes symbolic audio descriptions, while SKAD uses acoustic sketches and contrastive decoding to resist language-prior drift.

Audio-CoTSAR-LMSKAD
02 / SUPERVISION

Reasoning and Perception Alignment

SFT learns from plans, captions, summaries, or structured chains. Its value depends on whether intermediate annotations preserve accurate acoustic evidence.

03 / OPTIMIZATION

Adaptive and Audio-Grounded RL

RL methods adapt reasoning to problem difficulty, filter for audio dependence, and reward perceptual evidence rather than final-answer correctness alone.

SECTION VIII

Capabilities · metrics · protocols

Evaluations

Reasoning-capable AFMs require more than answer accuracy. Evaluation should test grounding, cross-modal alignment, streaming behavior, speech quality, and reliable execution.

VIII-A

Audio-to-Text

Separate content-based reasoning from acoustic-based reasoning, then use controlled changes to test whether an answer truly depends on audio.

VIII-B

Audio-to-Speech

Measure dialogue ability, speech quality, response alignment, real-time interaction, and subjective conversational quality.

SECTION IX

Open problems

Challenges and Future Directions

The paper closes the technical survey with six connected gaps. Each asks whether future AFMs preserve acoustic evidence while scaling reasoning, interaction, and action.

01

Reliable synthesized reasoning data

Generated traces must be tied to timing, speaker identity, prosody, and other verifiable acoustic evidence.

02

Grounding failures and shortcuts

Better perception is not enough. AFMs must also preserve acoustic evidence throughout long decoding.

03

Accuracy versus latency

Optimize reasoning jointly with Time-To-First-Audio, continuity, interruption recovery, and speech consistency.

04

Long-context audio reasoning

Retrieve sparse evidence across meetings, podcasts, and long videos without breaking temporal or cross-modal alignment.

05

Causal grounding and agent reliability

Intervene on audio, speakers, time, modalities, and tools, then measure every stage through final task completion.

06

Reasoning-oriented pre-training

Move grounding objectives earlier so later SFT or RL does not need to recover evidence discarded by the backbone.

SECTION X

Conclusion

Ground the evidence. Coordinate the reasoning. Complete the action.

This survey unifies four audio reasoning paradigms through their formulations, foundations, training, data, and evaluation.

Across them, effective reasoning depends on extracting acoustic evidence and preserving it from perception to response or execution.
